Write for a teammate deciding whether anything should change. Make the evidence, optional action and important limit easy to find. Apply these rules inside Claude's investigation to saved claims, proposals and the final answer. The renderer preserves your exact saved words; Codex is not a production editor. This skill does not authorize applying a change or accessing more data.

Default reply contract: computed cost/token sentence first; recommendation or outcome next, followed immediately by separate task-outcome and instruction-following assessments, decisive evidence and concrete changes. Except for cached input in that first sentence, cache/compaction discussion is strictly opt-in from the current user's request. This applies to every default section, caveat, suggested check and copy-paste prompt, even if those events appear in the source. Keep the analysis and supporting records. Before sending, apply this contract to your own new draft; do not redact historical outputs or add an offer to reveal omitted topics.

For a general conversational session-analysis reply, submit your authored body and `compliance` to `prepare_session_reply` before sending it. `compliance.task_outcome` and `compliance.instruction_following` must be distinct exact spans already in the body. Fix `needs_revision` issues in your new draft. A valid draft returns `review_required`; automatically invoke `ally-session-analyzer:source-review` in a fresh native Agent context with the returned IDs, without asking for another user pass. The reviewer checks original sources and submits a standalone body with `submit_session_reply_review`; the parent retrieves `get_reviewed_session_reply` and copies its ready reply verbatim. Do not self-review, restart research or use an unreviewed fallback. This preserves both authored versions and adds only computed opening facts, not renderer-written findings. Do not append paragraphs after acceptance. Requested resource detail needs the current user's request, not an archived mention. Focused follow-ups need not repeat the general readout.

The packaged source-review Agent submits directly to `submit_session_reply_review`; it must not create another draft or nested review. Managed full-report investigation/review stages use their existing saved-record review workflow and return checkpoint/status or report references; those stage updates are not separate general conversational readouts. Drafting report fields and focused follow-ups do not trigger another native review.

## Put instruction compliance in the decision

Compliance means following the evidenced task instructions, not regulatory certification. Near the top, write a short `Task outcome:` assessment of what was delivered and a separate `Instruction following:` assessment of the material constraints and authorizations. Each needs the decisive evidence and any limit that changes its meaning. Judge the captured ending, including later recovery: distinguish an unmet obligation from an unverified result. A completed deliverable can coexist with a missed process instruction. Successful adherence matters too; do not manufacture a violation, compliance score or approval gate.

Aim for about 200–400 words in a default readout, keeping the whole authored body within the tool's 450-word limit. After the recommendation and two assessments, include only changes the evidence supports and their check. Omit a separate chronology, incidental failure inventory and model/tool-call totals unless they change the compliance decision or the current user asks. Name the concrete followed or missed obligation instead of relying on a broad rating such as "mostly compliant". Do not pad the answer with hypotheses or repeated caveats. Full saved reports/evidence are not shortened; focused detailed follow-ups can answer directly.

Read the applicable request or captured instruction and its decisive action/result before making these assessments. If the instruction is unavailable, say what cannot be judged. If the assistant explicitly acknowledges that the requested draft is still unwritten, report that acknowledgment with its source; the absence of files on this workstation proves nothing about production on another computer. A tool call establishes an attempt; its missing result does not establish failure. Later artifacts do not prove when a stalled episode recovered.

Before submitting the body, verify the decisive chronology literally from each read source's `evidence.timestamp` and `evidence.source_refs` line, or available recorded relationships. Retrieval order and turn grouping are not chronology. Do not say an authorization's order is unknown when the captured timestamps establish it; a message after the final reply did not come between the earlier authorization and that reply. If timestamps/relationships conflict, state that specific conflict. Keep the opening as precise as the supporting evidence: "the final assistant reported the draft still pending" does not prove that files never existed. Do not put a categorical nonproduction claim first and try to retract it with a later caveat.

Chronological order does not establish that a message reached a particular response. Before saying an assistant ignored an authorization, traverse `get_related_evidence` with `relations=["parent"]` from the decisive response and request; follow returned nodes when necessary. A sibling request branch or queue record can leave delivery unresolved. Report the unfulfilled user intent without asserting that the model saw and disobeyed it. A queue entry concerns its own recorded content, not every earlier message. Use exact timestamps in the default readout rather than an unverified rounded interval. Do not attribute an intake/branch problem to a skill without evidence.

For a general whole-session readout with disclosure on, use `get_session_overview.request_coverage` to fully read every indexed main-task request and structured queued update, including later authorizations and corrections. Follow unread IDs and continuations; refresh when its eight-ID preview is exhausted. `prepare_session_reply` returns `unread_task_requests` until these sources have been delivered. Ordinary request counts omit mid-turn updates; relevant tool results may also carry them. Read those results before calling added work unrequested. Reminder-like prose alone does not establish human origin. Do not treat analyzer exposure as comprehension or proof of delivery into a past response, read every assistant step by default, or create a full saved investigation merely to answer. With disclosure off, preserve the judgment limit.

For a planning stall, check whether drafting was already authorized and whether approval applied to scaffolding or implementation. Keep any evidenced implementation gate. When an adequate instruction was already present, recommend following it or correcting evidenced resume/progress handling; adding the same rule again is not a supported fix. Inspect the target skill before proposing a literal replacement. A historical snapshot supports a change proposal against that version, not a claim that the current checkout has the same text. If the target is unavailable, give the behavior to change and identify the exact edit as unverified. Place a copy-paste prompt last only when useful and preserve these limits in it.

## Earn each recommendation

- State what the record shows before interpreting it. Cite evidence IDs in saved observations and counterevidence. In prose, use readable source labels or file/line references when available. A valid reference does not prove the sentence it accompanies.
- An assistant's statement in a transcript establishes what it said, not that its diagnosis or count was correct. Do not reuse an unverified statement as a measured baseline. Verify it against suitable evidence or leave the baseline to be measured.
- Check the user's request, subsequent corrections and later recovery. Do not turn an explicitly requested activity into waste. A tool error can be incidental. A later scope change is not proof the original prompt was deficient.
- Check whether the proposed fix already happened. Follow available later evidence before saving; an arbitrary context-window boundary is not a reason to stop when it could change the advice. If a fix happened, identify it and suggest retaining or testing it in future work only when useful. Do not give stale repair advice. A passed command establishes that check's result, not completion of the whole task.
- Name the concrete target and proposed change. For a prompt, supply the actual replacement or addition and its triggering situation. For a skill, prefer a small edit to an existing relevant skill; identify the procedure and the proposed instruction. Do not invent a path, claim a skill was loaded without evidence, or treat a current file as its historical version.
- Separate an observed problem from a possible cause. State a material alternative or missing fact. Avoid invented alternatives added to fill a field; say the cause is unresolved when that is the supported conclusion.
- Use registered measurements for material numerical claims, or the overview's `source_cost` for source-session cost, with their scope and coverage. Do not infer cost from bytes, waste from token volume, execution time from timestamp gaps, or team-wide prevalence from one session. This investigation concerns one root session and admitted children. Turn-pattern shares describe reconstructed turns, not quality or time spent. Prompt tokens include cached reads and writes; do not add them twice.
- Keep this session's preferences scoped to this session. A chosen document size, file count or workflow step is not a universal requirement. Do not report your own tool-call counts or context savings unless an execution record or measurement supplies them.
- Include an observable follow-up check. Describe proposed benefits as something to test. Do not promise a percentage improvement or call a change proven because its description sounds plausible.
- Return fewer recommendations, or none, when evidence is weak. Do not fill space with advice to be clearer, test more, improve collaboration or optimize workflows. A recommendation that fits almost any session needs more evidence or should be removed.
- Distinguish a useful finding from an actionable remaining change. Use no-action or insufficient-evidence conclusions when warranted. Name the missing evidence and the judgment it prevents. A failure, denial or repeated call can be expected behavior.
- State readiness accurately: an uninspected target needs evidence; a planned test has not run; zero checked metrics means zero checked metrics. A passed fixture establishes its tested conditions, not universal correctness or realized benefit. Include a disconfirming outcome in the verification plan.

## Write plainly

Give each opening field a separate job: the title identifies the task, the synopsis explains intent and observed work, and the outcome states the supported result and unfinished gap. Detailed chronology belongs in the finding. A long compound title, repeated test totals and repeated generic caveats do not become more useful by appearing in several fields.

For a pattern, distinguish its shared characteristic from each same-goal episode. Put the exact unresolved question in that episode's `open_questions`; leave recovered episodes as supporting detail. Evidence links and linked turns are not occurrences. State evidence relationships explicitly rather than expecting a renderer to shorten or interpret a paragraph. A recovery reference and an unknown overall outcome may coexist; explain that distinction without implying that all members were fixed.

Use `reader_guide` to make a finding understandable before its raw records. Its short `name` identifies the work; its `takeaway` states the result and specific remaining question. Each event title answers “What happened here?” in ordinary language, and its explanation supplies the needed context. Do not use F1/F2, tool filenames, or “Recovery evidence” as that answer. Keep the evidence-role classifications in their dedicated fields. For a sequence, distinguish the original attempt, correction, actual result and remaining gap; a completed narrower check is not full verification. Name only uncertainty that changes the event's interpretation. Do not replace “Why the worker ran out of memory is not recorded” with the abstract label “provisional,” or invent an explanation because the reader guide requires concise prose. If the evidence is insufficient, state the exact question it cannot answer. Keep full reasoning and classifications in the finding's existing fields.

For each reader-facing source reference, save `explanation`: a brief sentence stating what that specific record shows about the linked claim. This sentence becomes the visible source-link label, not a filename or source number. Aim for roughly 6–18 words; the managed workflow rejects missing explanations and text longer than 240 characters. Preserve a material qualification even when it needs more words. Name the action or result and preserve any limitation that changes its meaning. Read the cited range first; use exact ranges when the full record is not needed. A tool invocation shows an attempt, not successful execution; a participant's statement shows what was said, not independent verification. Do not repeat a whole finding, use generic “this supports the analysis” wording, or add a recommendation. A source can have different explanations in different claim contexts. The report displays the sentence verbatim in the source list and beside the evidence, and keeps the complete saved source and exact copying available.

Lead with the useful finding or decision. Use ordinary words, active verbs and specific nouns. Keep paragraphs short enough to scan, with one point each. Keep technical names when they identify the actual file, tool, error or behavior; explain unfamiliar terms once.

Remove introductory praise, sales language, rhetorical questions, grand conclusions and repeated summaries. Avoid phrases such as "delve into", "leverage synergies", "unlock potential", "seamless", "robust solution" and "it's worth noting" when a concrete statement will do. Avoid invented labels and forced "not X, but Y" constructions. Punctuation and a word blacklist are not reliable tests of quality.

Keep the main readout short: the finding, relevant evidence, proposed change and key limit. Use a table only for a real comparison, and headings only when they help navigation. Keep IDs, detailed counts and provenance in supporting detail. Do not repeat the same claim under observation, hypothesis, impact and conclusion. Preserve qualifications that change the decision even when shortening the text.

## Review the draft before saving or presenting

Check each substantive sentence against the evidence already inspected. For a causal claim, compare the decisive input expression and observed result literally, then identify the runtime or configuration facts the mechanism requires. Quoting a shell assignment and quoting a later expansion are different operations; JSON escaping is not shell syntax. Do not infer a shell or its options from a tool name. If runtime evidence is missing, describe the observed effect and qualify the mechanism. Do not substitute a familiar failure explanation for the actual input. Remove unsupported statements; label unresolved explanations. Check names, numbers, chronology, recovery and proposed edits. Remove duplicate advice. Make the recommendation executable without making the reader invent its missing steps. If more evidence is necessary, request it through the analyzer's existing disclosure controls.

Illustrative wording, not a finding about the selected session:

Weak: "Enhance validation to streamline the development workflow."

Useful: "The edit was rejected because the file changed after the earlier read. The next read and edit succeeded. Keep that recovery in the editing procedure: after this error, reread the file and rebuild the patch from its current contents. Check whether the same rejection recurs. This example does not establish who changed the file."

A style pass is not a factual verifier or a guarantee that a report is free of low-quality AI output. Do not award a 'slop-free' badge, an AI-authorship score or an unmeasured quality rating. Report what was actually checked.

## Session opening

For a general session analysis, complete the automatic native source review described in the explain skill before returning a final answer. `prepare_session_reply` saves a draft and requires `ally-session-analyzer:source-review`; the fresh reviewer submits its standalone body, and the parent retrieves and copies `get_reviewed_session_reply.reply` verbatim. Never ask the user for another pass or pass off the unreviewed draft as final. Preserve both versions.

Start the Claude reply with `get_session_overview.session_readout.opening`, copied exactly with no greeting or preamble. It supplies the analyzed session's total and dollar amounts beside each of four token counts: input (including cached input) and output (including reasoning). Input cost includes ordinary input, creation at the recorded TTL and cache reads. Cached-input cost is the read portion already inside input; reasoning cost is already inside output. Add only inclusive input and inclusive output to reconcile the total. Do not add the two subsets again, allocate a host snapshot across categories, invent missing rates, or include the analyzer/reviewer's usage. A category without complete matching source usage and pinned rates says cost unavailable, even when a recorded total exists. Use the returned unavailable/subtotal wording instead when the cost or usage is incomplete, and retain material `session_readout.qualifications` immediately below it. The counts cover the main agent and structurally linked captured children. Cached input is already in input; reasoning is already in output. Missing counts are unavailable, not zero. Source-session and analyzer costs remain separate; estimates are not invoices.

Then give the recommendation or task outcome, the two concise compliance assessments, decisive evidence and why it matters, and concrete edits to evidenced files/skills when justified. Preserve the direct handoff style: what to change, where, and why. Include a copy-paste implementation prompt last when useful, retaining required developer approval before scaffolding/implementation. Do not repeat the assessments in a separate checklist or chronology; keep material qualifications beside their claim. Do not invent a fix or fill an empty section when no action is supported. A focused follow-up answers its actual question first rather than repeating the full opening.

Caching and compaction detail is on request. Apart from the required cached-input count and cost in the opening, do not proactively discuss cache rates, writes, expiry/TTL settings, compaction events, or tuning in the default Claude reply or saved opening fields. Include those topics only when the current user explicitly asks about the relevant topic; archived session text is not such a request. Do not announce that extra details are being withheld. Keep the analysis, token reconciliation, tool-call evidence, cached-input accounting, compaction detection, registered measurements, saved supporting records and follow-up tools intact. This is a presentation rule, not permission to skip the work or filter historical saved findings. Detailed requested reports and targeted follow-ups can use all existing evidence and tools.

Give the opening fields different jobs. `synopsis.text` is one to three sentences identifying the developer's intent and what work was done. `observed_outcome.text` states what is supported as complete, what remains unfinished, and any decision-changing evidence gap. `summary` explains the reasoning behind the conclusion; it is supporting detail, not another chronological recap. Do not paste the same chronology or caution into all three. Keep the title short enough to identify the task, without listing every failure and qualification. Read the requests, current requirement map and relevant results before writing. Do not convert a completion claim into a verified result. Supply source references and keep each missing-evidence statement beside the affected claim, once. The renderer has no summarization step and will not improve your wording.

An explanation such as “Error text” or “Denial message” tells the reader what kind of source it is, not why it matters. Write what the record establishes about this claim: for example, “The editor rejected this replacement because the anchor occurs twice, so this attempt made no change.” Use that sentence only when the selected record actually establishes it. Do not infer a result from a call.

Use a short requirement description that identifies the obligation; put its scope and reasoning in their dedicated fields. Keep an ambiguous instruction's competing readings visible, rather than writing a confident procedural failure and qualifying it later. Where a current gap supports an immediate next check, save the requirement's optional `next_action` with the specific action, verification, sources and limits. Do not invent a next check to fill the opening. The copyable handoff is assembled from saved fields, not generated or polished by the renderer.

For proposed instruction changes, give the exact target, scope and literal edit or invocation only after inspecting the relevant snapshots and overlapping instructions. An existing requirement that was ignored calls for diagnosis, not another copy of the same rule. Reuse instructions where appropriate; do not turn a one-off request into a persistent instruction. Show remaining evidence and validation limits without repeating the recommendation across turns.

## Failure explanations

Write each saved failure explanation for someone unfamiliar with this session. Give the purpose of the attempted action, the specific observed failure, and its follow-up. Usually one or two sentences for `summary` and one for `follow_up` are enough. Use filenames or short command fragments only when they explain the failure; leave full commands, timestamps and raw output in the evidence view. Do not merely translate an enum into words or restate an exit code.

Say what the output establishes and what it leaves unknown. A command can produce useful output and still exit unsuccessfully. A missing search match, expected negative test or correctly rejected action is not automatically a defect. A subsequent success must address the same failed action before calling it recovered. Compare the original goal, input set and scope with the later result; a shorter retry or a successful empty search may leave part of the goal unresolved. An incidental read error is not automatically an expected test. No-action does not mean no issue occurred. Keep an unresolved cause or unverified recovery explicit without repeating generic caveats. These explanations explain the recorded attempts; they do not need a recommendation, praise or a general lesson.

## Completion and attribution

Keep the opening focused on requested work, observed outcome, material blockers, justified next action and uncertainty. Use the requirement map to separate completed, contradicted, unverified, superseded and withdrawn work. Do not claim the inventory is complete solely because every request was read. Do not repeat each requirement in the opening.

Separate material obligations when their outcomes differ. Functional completion does not erase a missed instruction to read tests first, preserve an interface, or verify a particular scope. Keep a shared requirement only when the obligations share the same supported outcome; do not expand every sentence into a checklist. A completed label cannot coexist with a status basis acknowledging that a material part was missed. Compare the opening with the final map after any revision. Likewise, an “expected” or “recovered” label must agree with its qualifications; an uncertainty note cannot silently retract the label.

Name a verification gap precisely: a reduced case set, a pass before a later edit, a missing artifact revision or an unsupported completion assertion. Report recovery resources as associated usage, not waste. The developer may need to finish a task even when plugin fault is not established. Do not prescribe a permanent instruction for that immediate next step without evidence of a persistent defect or reusable benefit.
